# EU-wide rules

Rules shared across the member states, for a business selling from one EU country into others,
or into the EU from outside. Researched 22/06/2026; the EU thresholds below are set in EU law and
were unchanged when the country files were checked on 16/10/2026. Reference, not advice.

## B2B services: the reverse charge

For most services to a VAT-registered business in another member state, VAT is due in the
customer's country and the customer accounts for it. The supplier invoices without VAT, adds a
reverse-charge mention and both parties' VAT numbers, and reports the sale on its EC sales list.

- **Check the customer's VAT number in VIES** (ec.europa.eu/taxation_customs/vies) before
  invoicing without VAT, and keep the result. If it is invalid, charge your own country's VAT or
  get a certificate from the customer.
- Exceptions (land, event admission, passenger transport, on-site catering) rarely affect a
  software or services business.

## B2C sales: the EUR 10,000 threshold and OSS

- A business **established in one member state** charges its own country's VAT on cross-border
  B2C sales of digital services and distance sales of goods while the **combined EU-wide total
  stays at or below EUR 10,000 a calendar year**. It is one figure across all countries, not per
  country. Above it, the customer's country's VAT applies, from the sale that crosses it.
- A business **established outside the EU** has no EUR 10,000 allowance: destination VAT from
  the first B2C sale.
- **One-Stop Shop (OSS):** one quarterly return in one member state declares and pays the VAT
  due to all the others, instead of registering in each. Union OSS for EU businesses, non-Union
  OSS for non-EU businesses selling services. Returns are due by the end of the month after the
  quarter.
- **IOSS:** for imported goods in consignments up to EUR 150, monthly.

For SaaS and other electronically supplied services sold to consumers, the place of supply is
where the consumer lives.

## The EU small-business scheme (from 2025)

From 01/01/2025 a business whose total EU turnover is at most EUR 100,000 may use another member
state's domestic small-business VAT exemption, under that state's threshold and rules, after
notifying its own tax authority. Each national file gives the domestic threshold.

## E-invoicing direction of travel: ViDA

The EU "VAT in the Digital Age" package (adopted 11/03/2025) lets member states mandate domestic
B2B e-invoicing without a special derogation, and makes structured e-invoicing and digital
reporting mandatory for **intra-EU** B2B from **01/07/2030**, with national systems converging by
01/01/2035. Building to the European standard EN 16931 now is forward-compatible with every
national mandate in this guide.

## GDPR baseline

The GDPR applies directly in every member state; each national file lists the local additions
(age of consent, DPO triggers, the regulator).

- **Fines:** up to EUR 20m or 4% of worldwide annual turnover for the most serious breaches;
  EUR 10m or 2% for others.
- **Breach notification:** to the supervisory authority within 72 hours where there is a risk to
  people; to the people affected without undue delay where the risk is high. Record every breach
  internally.
- **Records of processing** are effectively mandatory for most businesses: the under-250
  exemption falls away for regular or sensitive processing.
- **Transfers outside the EEA** need an adequacy decision, standard contractual clauses (the 2021
  set) or another safeguard. The EU-US Data Privacy Framework (adequacy decision of 10/07/2023)
  covers only certified US recipients and has faced legal challenge: keep SCCs as a fallback for
  US processors and re-check its status.
