# France

marked **(checked 16/10/2026)** were re-verified that day; everything else was researched on
22/06/2026. Corsica and the overseas departments differ and are out of scope. Reference, not
legal or tax advice.

## VAT (TVA)

- **Rates:** 20% standard (including software and SaaS); 10% (passenger transport,
  accommodation, restaurants, some renovation); 5.5% (most food, books, some energy works);
  2.1% (reimbursable medicines, press).
- **Franchise en base:** a France-established business below the prior-year threshold charges
  and reclaims no VAT: EUR 37,500 for services and liberal professions, EUR 85,000 for goods and
  accommodation (other bands exist for lawyers and authors). Crossing the tolerance threshold
  (EUR 41,250 or EUR 93,500) in the current year makes VAT due from the first day of the month of
  the overrun. A single EUR 25,000 threshold was legislated in 2025 and then abandoned; the
  figure still circulates online and does not apply.
- **Non-established businesses:** no threshold; in practice OSS (B2C) or the reverse charge
  (B2B) avoids French registration for most digital sellers ([eu-wide.md](eu-wide.md)).
- **Filing:** réel simplifié (annual CA12 with two instalments) up to EUR 286,000 services or
  EUR 945,000 goods; réel normal (monthly CA3, or quarterly if annual VAT is under EUR 4,000)
  above. All online via the professional space on impots.gouv.fr.
- **VAT number:** FR + 2-character key + 9-digit SIREN.
- **Late payment:** interest 0.20% a month plus a 5% surcharge; late or missing returns 10%, 40%
  or 80% depending on the circumstances.

## E-invoicing and e-reporting

Two obligations: **e-invoicing** (structured invoices between French VAT-registered businesses,
through an approved platform, a "plateforme agréée", formerly PDP) and **e-reporting**
(transaction and payment data for B2C and cross-border sales).

- **01/09/2026:** every French VAT-registered business must be able to **receive** e-invoices
  through an approved platform; large and mid-size companies (ETI) must also issue and
  e-report.
- **01/09/2027:** SMEs and micro-businesses must issue and e-report.
- The public portal is now only a directory and data hub; there is no free government exchange
  route, so every business needs an approved platform. Check the official list on impots.gouv.fr.
- **Formats:** Factur-X (PDF with embedded XML), UBL or CII, all EN 16931. New mandatory
  mentions include the buyer's SIREN, the delivery address if different, the category of
  operation (goods, services or mixed), and whether VAT is paid on debits.
- **Penalties:** EUR 50 per invoice not issued electronically and EUR 500 per missing e-reporting
  transmission, each capped at EUR 15,000 a year (raised from EUR 15 and EUR 250 by the 2026
  finance law). A first breach corrected within 30 days can be waived.

The dates moved repeatedly before (originally 2024). Re-check before relying on them.

## Company forms and registration

| | SAS / SASU | SARL / EURL |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minimum capital | EUR 1 | EUR 1 |
| Paid in at formation | at least 50%, rest within 5 years | at least 20%, rest within 5 years |
| Director's social regime | president is "assimilé salarié" (general scheme), no contributions if unpaid | majority manager is self-employed (TNS), minimum contributions even if unpaid |
| Dividends | no social contributions | TNS manager pays social contributions above 10% of capital |
| Raising investment | standard for venture | weaker |

The SA needs EUR 37,000. The micro-entreprise and the entreprise individuelle are sole-trader
regimes with no shares.

All formalities go through the single online portal run by INPI (guichet unique). Steps: draft
the statuts, deposit capital, publish a legal notice, file on the portal with the
beneficial-owner declaration, then receive SIREN, SIRET and the Kbis extract. Mandatory costs
are roughly EUR 300 before legal fees (practitioner figure).

## Corporate tax and the micro-entreprise

- **Corporate tax (IS):** 25%; 15% on the first EUR 42,500 of profit if turnover is under EUR
  10m, capital fully paid up and at least 75% held by individuals.
- **Micro-entreprise:** turnover ceilings EUR 83,600 (services) and EUR 203,100 (goods) for
  2026; social contributions as a flat share of turnover (about 21% to 26% for services). The
  VAT franchise thresholds are far lower than these ceilings, so a growing micro business usually
  charges VAT long before it leaves the regime.

## Employment

- **SMIC:** EUR 12.31/hour, EUR 1,867.02/month gross for 35 hours from 01/06/2026 (it rose on
  01/01/2026 and again mid-year after inflation passed 2%). Re-check each January and mid-year.
- **Working time:** 35-hour legal week (the overtime threshold, not a cap); overtime +25% for
  the first 8 hours, +50% beyond, unless an agreement says otherwise (never below +10%); maximum
  10 hours a day and 48 in a week. Many managers and engineers work on an annual day count
  (forfait-jours, up to 218 days).
- **Paid leave:** 2.5 working days a month, 5 weeks a year.
- **Employer cost:** roughly 1.25 to 1.45 times gross salary, depending on pay level and reliefs.
- **Hiring:** a pre-hire declaration (DPAE) before the first day; monthly payroll declaration
  (DSN); income tax withheld at source.
- **Collective agreements:** nearly every employer is bound by a sector agreement (identified by
  its IDCC number, printed on every payslip). For software and consulting it is commonly Syntec
  (IDCC 1486). It usually improves on the legal floor for notice, severance and pay.
- **Termination:** no at-will dismissal. Statutory severance after 8 months' service: a quarter
  of a month's pay per year for the first 10 years, a third beyond. The "rupture
  conventionnelle" is the usual agreed exit.

## Data protection

GDPR plus the loi Informatique et Libertés, enforced by the CNIL. French specifics:

- **Cookies:** refusing must be as easy as accepting, at the same level; no non-essential
  cookies before consent. The CNIL lists audience-measurement tools exempt from consent, under
  conditions.
- **Digital age of consent:** 15.
- **Health data** hosted for a third party needs HDS certification.
- **Employee monitoring** must be proportionate, announced in advance, and discussed with staff
  representatives.
- The CNIL can issue fines up to EUR 20,000 under a simplified procedure for simple cases; GDPR
  ceilings apply otherwise.
